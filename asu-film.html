ASU — Film Majors

The programs

Six ASU majors built around film development and its related fields — three schools, one institute (Herberger Institute for Design and the Arts) plus Liberal Arts and Sciences

1. BFA Film and Media Production — Poitier Film School — the production degree: directing, cinematography, post-production, screenwriting

2. BA Film (Filmmaking Practices) — Poitier Film School — screenwriters and industry professionals

3. BA Film (Film and Media Studies) — Liberal Arts and Sciences — analysis and criticism, not production; also online

4. Media Arts and Sciences BA / BS — The GAME School — digital and interactive media; has a Film concentration

5. BFA Art (Animation) — School of Art — animation concentration inside the art degree

6. MA Narrative and Emerging Media — Poitier + Cronkite — graduate, immersive media, Los Angeles

Curriculums below are ASU's own major maps and checksheets, copied from asu.edu — the catalog year is named on each card; where ASU's own pages conflict, the conflict is flagged in the card, not smoothed over

BA — Film, Filmmaking Practices (Poitier Film School)

Degree: Film (Filmmaking Practices), BA — Bachelor of Arts

Plan code: FAFPRBA

School: The Sidney Poitier New American Film School

College: Herberger Institute for Design and the Arts

Locations (Degree Search listing): Tempe, Los Angeles, Online, ASU Local

LA note: Los Angeles location = ASU California Center area; program page says LA accepts first-year students and transfer students with an associate degree in film or media production (or applicable credits) from Arizona and California community colleges

Arizona note (film.asu.edu): Arizona-based majors may be required to take classes on the Tempe Campus as well as at the ASU MIX Center in Mesa

Total credit hours: 120 minimum

Upper-division minimum: 46 hrs (2026-2027 checksheet)

First required math: MAT 114 College Mathematics, or any math course that meets the MATH designation; math intensity: General

What the degree centers on

School line (film.asu.edu): the BA in film with a concentration in filmmaking practices is for screenwriters and industry professionals (the BFA in film and media production is the one for directors, technicians and production-based careers)

Program focus: storytelling; art, craft and business of filmmaking; ethical and collaborative practices; project-based learning; journey of a story from idea to intended audience

Grounding (Herberger FAQ): fundamental production skills; entertainment and media ethics; feature, short and episodic screenwriting; general film and media industry history and practices

Key professional skills named: screenwriting for feature films, television and new media; producing practices — on-set production and production management, business and legal matters, professional and technical writing

Facilities: ASU MIX Center, Mesa (soundstages, color-grading room, sound-mixing studios, editing bays, screening theaters); California students: Los Angeles Center Studios

Senior project / capstone

All students: a year-long senior project, initiated and developed by the student, with dedicated faculty mentorship (Herberger FAQ; Barrett film page: BA Filmmaking Practices majors must create an independent senior project)

Current requirement (2026-2027 checksheet, Film Capstone): 3 semesters, 9 hrs, cannot be condensed into 2 semesters

Capstone sequence — FMP 397 Senior Thesis Development OR FMP 398 Capstone I: Development — 3 hrs

Capstone sequence — FMP 495 Senior Thesis I OR FMP 480 Capstone II: Production — 3 hrs

Capstone sequence — FMP 496 Senior Thesis II OR FMP 481 Capstone III: Post-Production — 3 hrs

Admission / portfolio gates

Portfolio: none listed for this BA — no portfolio or interview gate on the program page or checksheet

Selective gate nearby (not this degree): the selective, studio-based BFA in Film and Media Production has its own application/interview process; all film students enter as Filmmaking Practices majors first

Change of major (program page): new to this major — minimum cumulative GPA 2.25, and GPA must be brought up to 2.50 or higher within one semester to remain in good standing; returning to this major — minimum cumulative GPA 2.50

Film GPA requirement (2026-2027 checksheet): minimum 3.0 major GPA; minimum 2.5 ASU cumulative GPA

2020-2021 major map gates: maintain 2.50 GPA in Theatre and Film courses; minimum grade C in major courses; map totals line: major GPA 3.00 minimum, cumulative GPA 2.00 minimum

BFA — Art (Animation) (School of Art)

Degree: Art (Animation), BFA — Bachelor of Fine Arts in Art with a concentration in Animation

School: School of Art, Herberger Institute for Design and the Arts, Arizona State University

Campus: Tempe

Plan code: HIARTANBFA

Total credit hours: 120 (upper division: 45 minimum, per the map)

Minimum grade: C in all major courses shown on the map

First required math: MAT 114 College Mathematics (or any MATH-designation course); math intensity: General

Second language requirement: No

How the concentration is structured

Animation is a formal concentration inside the BFA in Art (concentration first offered catalog year 2020-21; established under the BFA Art degree, School of Art)

The major map is animation-specific from Term 1: students follow the Art (Animation) map from entry, starting with School of Art foundation courses; the first animation course, ART 217 Introduction to Computer Animation, comes in Term 2

Admission: general ASU university admission requirements only — the degree page lists no additional program admission requirements

Portfolio gate: none stated on the official degree page, the School of Art animation page, or the major map — no portfolio review is required for entry

Change of major: current ASU students have no additional requirements to change into this program

Structure by level: foundation + lower-division concentration courses in years 1-2 (drawing, digital media, 2D/3D animation, storyboarding); upper-division concentration courses in years 3-4 (Junior Animation Projects I-II, storyboarding/sound, Animation Capstone I-II); 15 hrs of upper-division Animation electives spread across Terms 5-8

Capstone: ART 461 Animation Capstone I + ART 462 Animation Capstone II (Terms 7-8) — capstone research courses build a portfolio of completed works
